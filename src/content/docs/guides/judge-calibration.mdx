---
title: "Judge Calibration"
description: "Check whether a model judge agrees with human labels before relying on its scores."
---

A model judge is a measurement, not a correctness oracle. Calibration compares its scores with human labels for the same rubric, provider and model. The judge subsystem is **beta**.

Use deterministic assertions for concrete requirements first. Use a judge for subjective criteria, then inspect agreement, repeated-sample variance and bias probes before making its scores a merge requirement.

## Keep measurements comparable

- Results identify the provider, model, rubric version and prompt hash.
- Repeated samples use the median of scored replies; abstentions and malformed replies are not fabricated scores.
- Calibration labels are scoped to provider and model as well as rubric, rubric version and prompt hash. Do not pool unrelated judges.
- Repeats and bias probes make additional judge calls; configure call and USD budgets deliberately.

In 1.1.0 a malformed reply is `not_evaluated(judge_malformed)` and rejected credentials are `not_evaluated(provider_auth)`, not low scores. Abstention also yields `not_evaluated`. The default policy only warns on these results; set `not_evaluated: block` when a required measurement must block the merge.

The [SDK judge guide](/sdk-guides/judges/) is the source for provider setup, local ACP safety, dynamic thresholds, budgets and [per-model calibration](/sdk-guides/judges/#calibration-is-per-model). The [verdict guide](/sdk-guides/verdicts/) explains test enforcement; [policies](/sdk-guides/policies/) explains merge enforcement.
